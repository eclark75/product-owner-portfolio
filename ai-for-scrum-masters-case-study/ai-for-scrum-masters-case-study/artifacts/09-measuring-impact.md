# 09 · Measuring the Impact of AI on the Team

The course covered **measuring impact and refining AI-enabled practices**. If we can't show that AI helps, we shouldn't keep using it.

## What we measure
| Measure | Why it matters | How we collect it |
|---|---|---|
| Refinement time per story | Is AI drafting actually saving time? | Scrum Master notes the time spent per item in refinement |
| % of stories Ready at Sprint Planning | Is the backlog better prepared? | Count against the Definition of Ready |
| Sprint Goal met (yes/no) | Is planning more realistic? | Sprint Review |
| Days waiting on dependencies | Are risks being caught earlier? | Board history |
| AI drafts accepted / edited / rejected | Are our prompts any good? | Quick tally in refinement |
| Team confidence in AI use (1-5) | Do people trust the process? | One question in the Retrospective |

## How to read it
- **High rejection rate** → improve the prompt, the context given or the constraints.
- **Time saved but quality down** → slow down; the drafts may be getting less review.
- **Low team confidence** → revisit the working agreement together.

## What this case study measured
This is a learning case study with a fictional team, so the measures above show **how I would track impact on a real team**. The examples in artifacts 02-06 show the kinds of changes to look for:
- One-line requests become sprint-ready stories after one refinement session.
- AI flags dependency risks early, so the team can escalate them before they block the Sprint Goal.
- Retrospective experiments come with a measure attached, so the team can tell whether they worked.
